The 10 best Google Meet attendance trackers, honestly ranked (2026)

Search "Google Meet attendance" and you'll find a graveyard: half the results are abandoned extensions, one charges people who can't figure out how to cancel, and one popular tool is shutting down in March. We build one of these tools, so read our ranking with that in mind — but every number below comes from public Chrome Web Store and Workspace Marketplace listings as of September 2026, and we tell you plainly where a competitor is the better pick.

What we tested for

  • Reliability across Meet updates. Extensions that scrape Meet's HTML break every time Google redesigns the UI. Official add-ons that read the Meet REST API don't.
  • Capture model. Does it only record while the host's tab is open? Does it merge rejoins, or count a flaky connection as three different people?
  • Exports. CSV is table stakes; Sheets, gradebook formats (Moodle/Canvas), and PDF matter for real classrooms.
  • Price honesty. Is the price stated up front, and can you actually cancel? One tool on this list has dozens of one-star reviews about exactly this.
  • Maintenance. Three attendance tools have died or been delisted since 2023, and another retires in March 2026. Update cadence is a survival signal, not a nicety.

The one structural fact that decides most of this list: browser extensions read the Meet participant list by scraping the page's HTML. That means they break when Google updates Meet's UI, they only capture while the host's tab is open, and they can't run at all on Firefox, Safari, or managed school Chromebooks. Official Workspace Marketplace add-ons run in Meet's Activities panel and read the official Meet REST API instead. Neither model is automatically "best" for you — but you should know which one you're buying.

1. Attendance Tracker — best overall

Ours, so here are the claims as verifiable facts rather than adjectives: it's an official Meet add-on (Activities panel, official Meet REST API — no DOM scraping), so it works in Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari, and on managed Chromebooks. The roster is real-time — Present, Late, Absent, and guests live during the meeting, not a post-meeting email. It merges rejoins instead of double-counting, shows full external email addresses, and has no 2-participant minimum. The free tier includes Class Rosters with one-click Google Classroom import, CSV/Excel downloads, per-session Moodle and Canvas gradebook CSVs, PDF reports, 3 Sheets exports a month, and a Meeting History dashboard. Pro is a $9.99 one-time lifetime pass for an individual (or $4.99/year for educators, or a $59/year Department plan for a whole school domain) — and adds unlimited Sheets exports, hands-free auto-capture of hosted meetings even when the panel wasn't open, semester roll-ups, per-attendee certificates with public verification, and Slack/Google Chat/Discord digests. It's translated into 30 languages and ships multiple updates weekly through 2026.

Caveats: it's organizer-focused (built for the person running the meeting), and the deepest features — auto-capture, semester roll-ups — are Pro.

2. Google Meet Attendance List (MeetList) — best if you require local-only storage

Blue Pixel Group's extension has earned its 4.7★ across 2,400 reviews and 400K users. It's free, clean, and stores everything on your device — nothing leaves your browser, which is a genuine privacy win and the reason some schools prefer it. The flip side of that design: no sync between devices, no team roll-ups, and it only records while the host's tab is open, so a crashed tab means a lost register. As an extension it's also Chrome-only and exposed to Meet UI changes. If local-only storage is a hard requirement, this is the tool we'd point you to.

3. Meets Attendance Registers — best if you just want the register emailed to you

Martin Molloy's add-on (464K+ installs) takes a deliberately different approach: it's built on Google Forms and Apps Script, and rather than an in-meeting panel it emails you the attendance register. If you want zero UI during class and a record in your inbox afterwards, that model is honest, free, and has served a lot of teachers for years. You give up real-time visibility — you can't see who's missing while the class is still running — and there's no live roster or gradebook formatting.

4. Meet Attendance Tracker (shaileshrkumar) — biggest install base

The most-installed tool in the category: 726K+ Marketplace installs plus a 100K-user Chrome extension at 4.2★. It's free and it covers the basics competently. The recurring complaint in reviews is cross-device sync — records captured on one machine not showing up on another. With only 21 Marketplace reviews against 726K installs, the rating signal is thinner than the install number suggests, but the install base itself says plenty.

5. AttendList — most polished subscription option

AttendList pairs an extension with a proper cloud backend, and the 4.8★ rating reflects real polish. Its pricing model is unusual and worth understanding: features are largely open, but history retention is gated — 14 days free, 60 on Pro ($9.90/month), 120 on Team ($29/month for 5 seats, which also unlocks the API). If you need an API and multi-seat cloud workflows and don't mind a subscription, it's a strong product. Over a school year, though, $9.90/month is ~$119 against a $9.99 one-time pass, and your older history is always aging out of reach.

6. Meeting Notes and Attendance — best self-check-in

Devfortress's add-on (296K+ installs, 41 reviews) has one genuinely distinctive feature: attendees can check themselves in. For webinars, trainings, or compliance settings where you want participants to actively confirm presence rather than be passively logged, that's a real differentiator no one else on this list offers. Notes and attendance in one tool is a sensible bundle for meeting-heavy teams.

7. Attendance for Google Meet (dllplayer) — solid free extension

40K users, 4.0★ across 467 reviews, free, and — importantly — updated as recently as July 2026. In a category where the most famous extensions were abandoned in 2023, "still being maintained" is a meaningful credential. It carries the usual extension limitations (Chrome-only, host-tab capture, exposed to Meet UI changes), but among pure extensions it's a trustworthy free pick.

8. Google Meet Enhancement Suite — best if attendance is a side dish

This is a bundle of 40+ Meet quality-of-life features — push-to-talk, auto-join, and attendance timestamps among them. If you were going to buy a Meet Swiss-army knife anyway, the attendance feature comes along for the ride (free tier available; Pro from $7/month). If attendance is the job, a dedicated tool will do it better: there's no roster matching, gradebook export, or history dashboard here.

9. Tactiq — attendance as a by-product of transcription

Tactiq is a transcription tool first: it captures the conversation and derives who attended and how long each person talked. If you want transcripts and talk-time analytics, attendance is a nice bonus. As a pure attendance tool it's the expensive route — per-seat subscription pricing for data a free tracker gives you directly — and there's no roster or gradebook concept.

10. RollCall — the minimalist newcomer

RollCall (meet-attendance.com) is small and new, free, and pitches local-only privacy much like MeetList. There's not yet enough track record or review volume to rank it higher, but it's a reasonable lightweight option if you want the simplest possible local capture. Honorable mention in the same spirit: Trackit — tiny (525 users, 4.0★) but free, open source, and it even handles in-person sessions.

Dead or dying tools to avoid

  • Meet Attendance (Clay Codes) — 500K users but 2.8★, not updated since February 2023, and a $2.50/month premium with a trail of one-star reviews about surprise charges and no way to cancel or remove a card ("I thought its free?"). Whatever it once was, don't put a payment method into an abandoned tool.
  • Attendance for Google Meet (Tyler and Adit) — 30K users, open source, but abandoned since April 2023; the classic symptom is the "first join a meeting" error shown while you are, in fact, in a meeting.
  • Attendance Collector — delisted from the Chrome Web Store in September 2025.
  • Meet Assistant — unmaintained since 2021 and incompatible with modern Chrome.
  • Attendance Taker for Classroom — its Marketplace listing says it's being retired on March 1, 2026 and will stop functioning. If you use it, here's a migration guide — export your data before the shutdown.

Frequently asked questions

What's the difference between an extension and an add-on?

A Chrome extension installs into your browser and reads the Meet page's HTML to see who's present; it breaks when Google changes Meet's layout and can't run on Firefox, Safari, or managed Chromebooks. A Workspace Marketplace add-on installs into your Google account, runs inside Meet's Activities panel, and reads attendance from Google's official Meet REST API.

Which tracker is best for teachers using Google Classroom?

Attendance Tracker is the only tool on this list with one-click Google Classroom roster import plus Moodle/Canvas gradebook CSV exports, so students are matched to your real class list automatically and grades land in your LMS without retyping.

Are free Google Meet attendance trackers safe?

Mostly, but check two things: when it was last updated (abandoned tools break silently and, in one case above, kept charging users), and what permissions it asks for. Prefer tools that state their storage model plainly — local-only like MeetList, or an audited Marketplace add-on.

Do any of these work without keeping a tab open?

Extensions can't — they only see what's in the host's open tab. Attendance Tracker's Pro auto-capture records meetings you host server-side, even if you never opened the panel.
