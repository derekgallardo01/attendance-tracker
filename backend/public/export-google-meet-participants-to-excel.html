Attendance Tracker

How to export a Google Meet participant list to Excel

Need your Meet attendance in a spreadsheet — names, emails, who was late, who left early, exact join and leave times? Here is the one-click way to get a CSV that opens straight in Excel, free.

Excel-ready, accents and all: the downloaded CSV is UTF-8 with a BOM prefix, which is the encoding Excel actually respects — so names like José, Zoë, and Nguyễn render correctly the moment you double-click the file. No Data → Import wizard, no mojibake.

Why you can't do this from Meet alone

Google Meet has no "export participants" button. The People panel disappears when the call ends, and attendance CSVs are emailed to hosts only on certain paid Education and enterprise Workspace editions. For everyone else, getting the list into Excel means tracking it yourself — which is exactly what the Attendance Tracker add-on does from the Meet side panel, using the official Google Meet REST API (no screen scraping, works in Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari, and on Chromebooks).

Step-by-step: Meet → CSV → Excel

Step 1: Open Attendance Tracker during your meeting

  1. Install the add-on once from the Google Workspace Marketplace.
  2. In your meeting, open the Activities menu and pick Attendance Tracker.
  3. The panel tracks everyone automatically — who's present, who joined late, who left early, and every join/leave timestamp.

Step 2: Click the CSV download

Hit the CSV download in the panel — during the meeting or right at the end. The file downloads instantly with one row per participant. (If the meeting already ended with the panel open, the report was exported automatically; you can also re-download it later from Meeting History.)

Step 3: Open it in Excel

Double-click the file. Because CSV is Excel's native interchange format, it opens as a clean worksheet with these columns:

Prefer a native .xlsx file? Go via Google Sheets

If you want a true .xlsx workbook rather than a CSV, use the Google Sheets export instead: one click sends the attendance table to a Sheet in your Drive, then in Sheets choose File → Download → Microsoft Excel (.xlsx). You get real cell formatting and a file that keeps formulas if you add any. Sheets exports are free 3 times per month; the CSV download is always free and unlimited.

Grading in an LMS instead?

If the spreadsheet's final destination is a gradebook, skip Excel entirely — the add-on also produces import-ready per-session CSVs for Moodle and Canvas, with the exact column layouts those systems expect.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need Excel installed for this to work?

No. CSV is a standard format — the same file opens in Excel, Google Sheets, LibreOffice Calc, Apple Numbers, or any tool that reads spreadsheets. "Opens in Excel" is simply the default double-click behavior on machines that have it.

Is the Excel export free?

Yes — the CSV download is part of the free tier, with no export cap. Google Sheets exports are free 3 times per month; unlimited Sheets exports (and hands-free Auto-Capture) come with Pro, from a one-time $9.99 Individual Lifetime Pass.

Can I export a meeting after everyone has left?

If the panel was open during the meeting, yes — the report auto-exports at meeting end and stays available in Meeting History. Pro Auto-Capture also picks up meetings you hosted with the panel closed, within a few hours, for meetings that ended in the last 2 days. What no tool can do is recover a meeting that ended before the add-on was installed — details here.
