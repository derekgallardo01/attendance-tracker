The columns every online class attendance template needs

Whether you build the sheet yourself or let software do it, a defensible attendance record needs more than a name and a checkbox. Here is the layout that holds up when a student, parent, or administrator asks questions later:

ColumnWhy it matters
NameThe student's display name, matched against your roster.
EmailThe stable identifier — names change, get abbreviated, or repeat; emails don't.
StatusPresent / Late / Left Early / Absent / Excused / Guest — a plain checkmark can't capture the difference between "showed up on time" and "arrived 20 minutes in."
Attendance %Minutes attended ÷ class length. The fairest single number for participation grades.
Duration (min)Total active minutes, so a 5-minute drop-in doesn't count like a full session.
Join TimeProof of when they actually arrived — the basis for late flags.
Leave TimeCatches early leavers, not just late arrivers.
RejoinsDistinguishes an unstable connection (many rejoins, honest attendance) from someone who left and came back at the end.
NotesRoom for context: excused reasons, tech issues, make-up work.

The problem with any manual template

A template solves the formatting problem. It does nothing about the real cost of attendance: collecting the data. With a manual sheet you still have to call roll (or scan the People panel), notice who arrived late, notice who quietly left at minute 40, and type it all in — every single session. In a class of 30+ students on Google Meet, that's minutes of lost teaching time and a record that's only as accurate as your peripheral vision.

And Google Meet itself won't fill the template in for you: unless your school pays for a specific premium Workspace edition, Meet keeps no attendance report you can access at all.

When a manual template still makes sense

Honesty corner: if you teach in person, or on a platform other than Google Meet, an add-on that reads Meet's roster can't help you — build the nine-column sheet above in Google Sheets and fill it in by hand or with a form. A manual template is also fine for a one-off meeting with five people, where roll call takes ten seconds. The automation earns its keep when classes are online, recurring, or big enough that watching the participant list becomes a job of its own.
