Google Meet vs. Zoom attendance reports (2026 comparison)

Organizations switching from Zoom to Google Meet often hit an unexpected roadblock: while Zoom provides usage and attendance reports across all paid tiers, Google Meet gates native attendance tracking behind its most expensive enterprise and education tiers.

The Good News: You do not need to pay for Google's most expensive license. With the free Attendance Tracker add-on, you get full Zoom-style attendance reporting directly within Google Meet and Google Drive.

How Attendance Tracking Differs: Google Meet vs. Zoom

1. Zoom Attendance Reports

Zoom generates post-meeting reports under Account Management > Reports > Usage Reports. It provides participant names, join/leave times, and durations. However:

  • Reports require navigating deep into the Zoom web dashboard after the meeting.
  • Data must be manually downloaded as raw CSV files.
  • Live in-meeting participant rosters with duration tracking are not visible during the call.

2. Native Google Meet (Built-In)

Google Meet has a built-in attendance feature, but it is heavily restricted:

  • Only available on Google Workspace for Education Plus, Teaching & Learning Upgrade, and Enterprise.
  • Requires a minimum of 5 attendees to trigger a report.
  • Only emails the CSV report to the original meeting creator.

3. Google Meet + Attendance Tracker Add-On (Best of Both Worlds)

Installing Attendance Tracker gives you the best features of both platforms:

  • Works on All Google Accounts: Free on personal Gmail, Google Workspace Starter, Standard, and Education Fundamentals.
  • Real-Time In-Call Side Panel: View active participants, join times, and late-arrival flags live while the meeting is ongoing.
  • Direct Google Drive Sync: Automatically creates tabbed, formatted Google Sheets without having to download or import CSVs.
  • Multi-Meeting Trends: Automatically rolls up attendance streaks across recurring standups, sprint reviews, or class lectures.
