Attendance Tracker

Google Meet attendance certificates with public verification

Run a webinar, CPE course, or training on Google Meet and every attendee wants proof they were there. Here is how to generate a personalized PDF certificate for each of them — backed by real tracked attendance and a verification code anyone can check.

Not just a pretty PDF: every certificate carries a unique verification code, and anyone — a registrar, a state bar, an employer — can confirm it's genuine at attendancetracker.dev/verify. Public, no account needed. Very few attendance tools offer independent verification at all; it's the difference between a certificate and a claim.

What gets generated

Attendance Tracker produces one PDF certificate per attendee, built from the attendance actually recorded during the meeting — not from a sign-up list. Each certificate includes:

FieldWhere it comes from
Attendee nameThe tracked participant's name from the meeting
Session titleYour meeting's title
DateThe session date
Duration / credit hoursActual minutes attended, or the credit hours you assign
Verification codeA unique code tied to that attendee and session

Because certificates are generated from join/leave data, someone who dipped in for five minutes of a two-hour course doesn't get the same paper as someone who sat the whole session — the duration on the certificate reflects what happened.

The verification story

Certificates are easy to forge; verifiable certificates aren't. Every certificate's code can be entered at attendancetracker.dev/verify — a public page, open to anyone, no login. It confirms the certificate is authentic and matches the recorded session. That matters wherever a certificate is actually inspected:

  • CPE/CLE audits: licensing bodies can spot-check credits themselves instead of taking a PDF at face value.
  • Employers and HR: confirm a candidate's training claim in ten seconds.
  • Schools and registrars: validate volunteer hours or make-up sessions without emailing the organizer.

How to generate certificates

  1. Track the meeting. Open Attendance Tracker from Google Meet's Activities panel during your session (it's an official add-on using the Google Meet REST API — works in Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari, and on Chromebooks). Attendance records automatically: who joined, when, and for how long.
  2. Export the session. When the meeting ends, the attendance report exports; the session appears in your Meeting History.
  3. Click Certificates. In the panel's link bar, choose Certificates — one PDF per attendee is generated, each with its own verification code, ready to distribute.

It works the same for 8 attendees or 300 — bulk generation is one click either way.

Who uses attendance certificates?

  • CPE & CLE providers: accountants, lawyers, and other licensed professionals need documented, auditable credit hours — see the CPE/CLE guide.
  • Webinar hosts and trainers: a certificate is the deliverable attendees actually keep and share — webinars & training guide.
  • Corporate L&D and compliance: proof of completion for mandatory training, with durations HR can defend.
  • Volunteer coordinators: verified hour counts that schools and scholarship programs accept — volunteer hours guide.
Full detail behind every certificate: the underlying session report keeps Name, Email, Status, Attendance %, Duration, Join/Leave times, and Rejoins for every participant — so if anyone questions a certificate, you have the timestamped record in Excel to back it up.
